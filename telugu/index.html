Telugu script descended from the Brahmi script (3rd century BCE), evolving through the medieval period into its modern form. The earliest confirmed Telugu inscription dates to 575 CE (Renati Cholas era). The script standardized by the 7th-8th centuries during the Chalukya dynasty, creating the distinctive rounded letterforms that characterize Telugu today. In 2008, India's government officially recognized Telugu as a Classical Language, acknowledging its 1,500+ year literary tradition alongside Sanskrit, Tamil, Kannada, and Odia. Modern Telugu orthography has been stable for centuries, with minor reforms during British colonial rule and independent India's linguistic standardization efforts.

Telugu uses an abugida script (like Kannada and Devanagari) — each character represents a consonant-vowel pair with inherent 'a'. Conjunct consonants (ligatures) create 100+ cluster combinations. Vowel diacritics (matras) attach above, below, or sideways and are visually complex. Telugu is highly phonetic with syllable-timed rhythm (not stressed-timed like English). Inside the Telugu Abugida

Telugu uses an abugida where each character = consonant + inherent 'a' vowel, plus 16 vowel diacritics (matras) and 100+ conjunct ligatures for consonant clusters — one of the most complex scripts for typing.

Inherent vowel

Every consonant has 'a'

కకాకికీకు

Telugu abugida: base consonant కhas built-in 'a' sound (ka). Add matras to change vowels: కా (kaa), కి (ki), కీ (kii), కు (ku). This principle differs from Latin where consonants and vowels are separate.

One consonant letter = multiple vowel sounds via matras
Vowel diacritics

Matras reshape sounds

ాిీుూ

Telugu has 16 vowel diacritics (matras): ా (aa), ి (i), ీ (ii), ు (u), ూ (uu), ె (e), ే (ee), ొ (o), ో (oo), ౌ (au), etc. Matras attach above, below, or sideways of consonants.

Matras are not optional — changing them changes word meanings
Conjunct ligatures

Consonant clusters merge

క్ య= క్య

Telugu creates 100+ conjunct ligatures (ligatures) for consonant clusters. Example: క + ్ (virama) + య = క్య (kya). These clusters are represented as single merged characters, not sequential letters.

Conjuncts make Telugu typography visually complex
Visual confusion

Not Kannada! (similar, not same)

కvsಕ

Telugu and Kannada scripts look visually similar (both abugidas, both Dravidian) but are completely distinct writing systems. Confusing them produces gibberish. Each language requires its own script.
