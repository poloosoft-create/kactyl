InScript, Shruti and the legacy fonts still in circulation

Gujarati typing is less fragmented than Hindi or Bengali, but it carries the same underlying split between real text and font-shaped text.

InScript is the standard layout for Unicode Gujarati and the one the Indian government specifies. It shares its design logic with the other Indian InScript layouts: vowels on the left, consonants on the right, alphabetical order rather than frequency order. Learn it for one Indian script and the shape of it transfers to the others.

Shruti is the Unicode Gujarati font Windows ships, and it is doing more work than it appears to. Conjunct forms are not typed directly — you type consonant, virama, consonant, and substitution tables inside the font decide which single joined shape to draw. Conjuncts of three or more consonants need a virama between each one. The virama sits on the apostrophe key.

Terafont and the other legacy Gujarati fonts are the equivalent of Hindi's Kruti Dev: still used in print and design work, still requiring dedicated input software, and still producing text that is not Gujarati underneath. Anything typed that way has to be converted before it can be searched, posted or sent.

This test reads Unicode, so InScript, phonetic input and the on-screen keyboard below all work. Legacy-font input does not.

What to do with a formal word list

The vocabulary here comes from published Gujarati, and that shapes how to use it. The connectives and reporting verbs that dominate news writing are exactly the words you will type most if you work in Gujarati professionally, so the list is well matched to office and study use and poorly matched to chat.

The key to watch is the virama, sitting on the apostrophe. It is the busiest key in Gujarati typing and the one least likely to be where your hand expects it, because no Latin layout puts anything important there. Every conjunct runs through it, and a three-consonant conjunct needs it twice.

If you have typed Devanagari before, budget time for ળ specifically. Gujarati uses it and Hindi does not, so a Hindi-trained hand has simply never been asked for that key. It will show up on your missed-character list long after the rest of the board feels automatic.

A note on where these words came from

Worth stating plainly, because it affects what you are practising on. These 600 words are drawn from a Gujarati news corpus, ordered by how often each one actually appears. The practical effect is that the vocabulary leans towards the register of published writing: reporting verbs, institutional nouns, the connectives that hold formal sentences together. That is good practice material if you type Gujarati for work or study, and slightly formal if what you want is conversational speed.

It also means a handful of words reflect what Gujarati newspapers were writing about when the corpus was collected. Frequency lists are photographs of a moment, not neutral samples of a language, and no typing test that uses one is exempt from that.

The same list feeds the autocomplete on our Gujarati keyboard, so the two agree with each other by construction rather than by anyone remembering to keep them in step.
