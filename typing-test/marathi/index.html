Marathi shares Hindi’s layout and almost nothing else

The Devanagari InScript key mapping is identical for Hindi and Marathi. Same keys, same positions, and the Marathi-specific characters sit within that same layout rather than displacing anything. A Marathi typist can work on a Hindi machine and the other way round, which is genuinely useful and also slightly misleading — it encourages the assumption that the two are interchangeable for practice purposes.

They are not, and the word lists are where it shows. Marathi words in this list average 5.34 codepoints against Hindi's shorter average, and Marathi carries ळ, ॲ and the ऱ्य / ऱ्ह forms that Hindi does not use at all. Practising Marathi speed on a Hindi passage trains you on the wrong letter distribution and skips the characters most likely to slow you down.

That is the reason this page exists separately rather than pointing at the Hindi test with the labels changed. The keyboard is the same; the language is not.

Outside InScript, Marathi typing meets the same legacy situation as Hindi: typewriter-era layouts paired with non-Unicode fonts, still in use in some offices, producing text that only displays correctly where the font is installed.

Practise for ten minutes at 95%, not for thirty seconds at speed

GCC-TBC decides what good practice looks like for Marathi, and it is not what most typing advice assumes. The exam is a ten-minute passage held at 95% accuracy. That is an endurance and control exercise. A candidate who can hit a high number for thirty seconds and then drift to 92% over ten minutes fails, and no amount of sprint practice fixes that.

So set this test to two minutes and treat accuracy as the target and speed as the byproduct. Watch the accuracy figure, not the words-per-minute one. When two minutes holds comfortably above 95%, push the speed a little and let accuracy fall back — then rebuild it. That cycle is slower to feel rewarding than chasing a peak number and it is what the exam actually measures.

The characters to drill are the ones Hindi practice material never gave you: ळ, ॲ, and the ऱ्य and ऱ्ह sequences. They are standard in Marathi and absent from Hindi, so if you learned on Hindi passages they are still untrained no matter how high your Hindi score is.

GCC-TBC, and what it actually asks for

Maharashtra's typing certification is the GCC-TBC — the Government Certificate in Computer Typing Basic Course, issued by the Maharashtra State Council of Examinations in Pune. It is offered in Marathi, Hindi and English, and it is graded at two speeds rather than one: 30 words per minute and 40 words per minute.

The details that matter for how you practise: accuracy is held at 95%, the speed passage runs for ten minutes, and Marathi is typed on the InScript layout with a Unicode font. The speed passage is only part of the paper — it carries 40 marks, sitting alongside an objective section and a practical section covering letters, statements and formatting.

Ten minutes at 95% is a different exercise from a thirty-second sprint. Sustained accuracy is the constraint, not peak speed, and the way to train for it is longer runs with fewer errors rather than short fast ones. Set this test to two minutes and aim to keep accuracy above 95% for the whole run before you try to push the speed number up.

The Marathi characters that cost time

ळ

The retroflex lateral

Marathi and Gujarati use it; Hindi does not. If you trained on Hindi material this key never came up, and it appears often in ordinary Marathi words.

ऱ्य

The eyelash ra forms

ऱ्य and ऱ्ह are built sequences, not single keys, and they are the standard stumbling block in GCC-TBC drills.

ॲ

The Marathi candra vowel

Used for the English-derived vowel in words like ॲप. Absent from Hindi practice material entirely.

्

Virama density

96% of the words in this list contain at least one cluster that takes more keystrokes than it displays — the highest proportion of the three Devanagari languages tested here.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Is this just the Hindi test with Marathi labels?
No. The word list is built from a Marathi corpus, with Hindi function words explicitly filtered out — Marathi news text quotes Hindi, and an unfiltered list would have carried it straight in. The layout is shared; the vocabulary is not.
How is the speed calculated?
Correctly typed whole words per minute, with visual letter units per minute shown beside it. On this word list that convention would report roughly 1.9 times the words-per-minute figure shown here, because it counts the keystrokes hidden inside conjuncts as if they were separate letters.
Can I practise for GCC-TBC here?
For the speed passage, usefully — set two minutes, hold accuracy above 95%, and build from there. The full GCC-TBC paper also covers theory and formatting, which no typing test covers.
Which speed should I aim for?
GCC-TBC certifies at 30 WPM and 40 WPM. Which one you need depends on the post you are applying for, so check the notification rather than assuming the higher grade.
Do I need Marathi installed?
No. The on-screen Devanagari keyboard below is clickable.
Why is my Marathi speed lower than my Hindi speed?
Marathi words in this list are genuinely longer — 5.34 codepoints and 2.87 visual units on average. More keystrokes per finished word.
Is Marathi InScript different from Hindi InScript?
The key mapping is the same. What differs is which keys you end up using, because ळ, ॲ and the ऱ्य forms appear in Marathi and not in Hindi.
What is the longest word in the list?
पार्श्वभूमीवर, at 6 visual units.
Does accuracy matter more than speed?
For GCC-TBC, measurably yes — 95% is the floor, and a fast run below it is a failed run. The result card here shows which characters you missed.
Does it work on a phone?
Yes, with your phone keyboard.
Is my score sent anywhere?
No. Your best stays in your browser.
How many distinct letter shapes does the list use?
304, counting each conjunct as its own shape.
