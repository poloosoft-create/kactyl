What Makes the Tamil Alphabet Distinctive

These are the features of the Tamil script that trip up learners coming from the Latin alphabet — the things worth understanding before you start reading or typing:

  • Tamil has an unusually small consonant set — just 18 — with no separate letters for voiced or aspirated sounds. க can be “k”, “g” or “h” depending on its position in the word; you infer the sound from rules, not from spelling.
  • Tamil famously has three different “L” sounds (ல, ள, ழ). The rarest, ழ (ḻa), is a curled retroflex approximant found in very few languages — and it sits in the language’s own name, தமிழ் (Tamiḻ).
  • It’s one of the world’s oldest living languages, with literature over 2,000 years old; the script descends from Brahmi through Tamil-Brahmi and Vaṭṭeḻuttu.
  • The 12 vowels combine with the 18 consonants (plus the āytham ஃ) to build the 247 characters of the full Tamil syllabary.
  • Unlike Sanskrit-based scripts, Tamil avoids stacked conjunct clusters, keeping its letters cleaner and more separated on the line.

Tamil Script — The Facts

  • Unicode block: Tamil, U+0B80–0BFF
  • ISO 15924 script code: Taml (346)
  • Script family: Brahmic abugida (Tamil script)
  • First attested: Tamil-Brahmi ~3rd century BCE
  • Speakers: 80 million (Ethnologue)

Sources: Unicode Tamil block (U+0B80) · Ethnologue — Tamil · ISO 15924 — Taml

Did You Know? Tamil is one of only two languages in the world recognized as a Classical Language by both India (2004) and Singapore (where it's an official national language). The other is Sanskrit. Tamil's classical status reflects its extraordinary literary heritage spanning more than two millennia.

About the Tamil Language

Tamil is one of the world's oldest living languages with over 2,000 years of written literature. It is spoken by 80 million people in Tamil Nadu (India), Sri Lanka, Singapore, and Malaysia. Tamil is the only classical language with an unbroken literary tradition from antiquity to the present day. Tamil — The Language That Never Died

Tamil has been continuously written and spoken for over 2,000 years — making it one of the world's longest-surviving languages. The Sangam literature from 300 BCE describes urban life, love, war, and nature in Tamil that modern speakers can still read with some study. Today, Tamil is a living, thriving language spoken across four countries. The Tamil diaspora — particularly the Sri Lankan Tamil community, the Malaysian Indian community, and Tamils in the UK, US, Canada, and Australia — actively maintains Tamil literacy online. Frequently Asked Questions

Why does Tamil have only 18 consonants?+
Tamil doesn't use separate letters for voiced or aspirated sounds the way Sanskrit-based scripts do. One letter covers several sounds — க can be k, g, or h — and you infer which from its position, so far fewer letters are needed.
What is the ழ (ḻa) sound?+
ழ is a rare curled retroflex approximant found in very few languages. It even appears in the language's own name, தமிழ் (Tamiḻ), and is one of three distinct “L” sounds in Tamil (ல, ள, ழ).
Why can one Tamil letter sound different in different words?+
Tamil spelling is economical: க is “k” at the start of a word but softens to “g” or “h” between vowels. The sound follows pronunciation rules rather than being spelled out with separate letters.
How many characters does Tamil actually have?+
The base set is 12 vowels + 18 consonants + the āytham (ஃ), but combining every vowel with every consonant produces the full syllabary of 247 characters.
