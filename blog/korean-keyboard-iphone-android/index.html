Korean Keyboard for iPhone & Android: Cheonjiin vs QWERTY

Quick Answer The easiest way to type Korean on iPhone or Android is to use kactyl.com — open it in your phone browser, type your text, then copy and paste it into any app. No app download or system settings required.

You don't need to install a Korean keyboard or change any phone setting to type Korean on a phone. Open kactyl.com/korean/ in your browser, tap the letters, copy, and paste into any app. But the on-device options are worth understanding too:

Cheonjiin (10-Key) vs QWERTY-Hangul

Korean phones offer two styles: the 10-key Cheonjiin (천지인), which builds vowels from three strokes (ㆍ ㅡ ㅣ) and is thumb-friendly, and a full QWERTY-Hangul layout. They feel completely different. Kactyl shows the jamo directly, so you can compose 한글 without committing to either.

Blocks Assemble as You Tap

Tapping ㅎ ㅏ ㄴ auto-combines into 한 — but mistap a final consonant and the whole block reshapes. Kactyl composes the same way with the letters visible, so you see the block forming before you paste it into a chat.

Typing Double Consonants (ㄲ ㄸ ㅃ ㅆ ㅉ)

Korean's tense consonants — ㄲ, ㄸ, ㅃ, ㅆ, ㅉ — are made by holding Shift (or long-pressing the plain consonant) on a phone keyboard, which beginners miss. Kactyl exposes them directly so words like 오빠 and 있다 are easy to build.

Frequently Asked Questions

Cheonjiin (10-key) or QWERTY-Hangul — which should I use?+
Cheonjiin builds vowels from three strokes and is thumb-friendly; QWERTY-Hangul is a full layout. They feel very different. Kactyl shows the jamo directly, so you needn't commit to either.
How do blocks assemble as I tap on mobile?+
Tapping ㅎ ㅏ ㄴ combines into 한; a mistapped final consonant reshapes the whole block. Kactyl composes the same way with the letters visible.
Can I type Korean in Latin instead?+
Not naturally — Koreans don't send “annyeong” for 안녕. A Hangul keyboard or Kactyl is the way.
Do I need to install a Hangul keyboard?+
Not with the browser method — type on kactyl.com and paste.
Will 한글 show correctly on any phone?+
Yes — standard Unicode renders identically everywhere.
