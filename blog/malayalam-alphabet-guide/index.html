About the Malayalam Script

Malayalam script evolved from Brahmi/Grantha scripts around 12th-13th century CE. Early Malayalam texts appear in copper plates (~9th century) but formal script standardization occurred 12th century onward. Medieval period (14th-18th centuries) saw classical Malayalam literature development (Cherusseri, Kumaran Asan). European colonial period (1800s) influenced orthographic standardization for educational purposes. British colonial administration standardized Malayalam orthography 1870s-1950s. Post-independence (1947), Malayalam Academy formalized modern script conventions. Contemporary Malayalam Unicode standardized 1998, enabling consistent digital rendering across devices.

What Makes the Malayalam Alphabet Distinctive

These are the features of the Malayalam script that trip up learners coming from the Latin alphabet — the things worth understanding before you start reading or typing:

  • Malayalam has one of the largest alphabets of any Indian language — 15 vowels and 36 consonants, plus a huge stock of conjunct ligatures — which gives its text its dense, rounded look.
  • As a Dravidian language that absorbed enormous Sanskrit vocabulary, it kept both the full aspirated Sanskrit series and the Dravidian extras (ള ḷ, ഴ ḻ, റ ṟ) — the biggest sound set of the South Indian scripts.
  • It shares the rare curled ഴ (ḻa) sound with Tamil — and it appears in the language’s own name, മലയാ‌ളം.
  • A reformed script introduced in the 1970s–80s simplified many stacked conjuncts for printing and typing, so older books look noticeably different from modern text.
  • Malayalam and Tamil are close cousins, but Malayalam’s letters are rounder and it writes far more Sanskrit-style consonant clusters.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is Malayalam script?+
Malayalam is an abugida script (consonant-vowel writing system) evolved from Brahmi/Grantha ~12th-13th century. Modern standardized form adopted post-independence (1947). Uses 36 consonants, 15 vowels, complex "chillaksharams" (standalone consonants), and highly stylized conjuncts.
What are Malayalam chillaksharams?+
Chillaksharams are standalone consonants without inherent vowel, unique to Malayalam (unlike Tamil/Kannada). They appear at word-end or before certain consonants, creating Malayalam's distinctive visual signature.
How many Malayalam speakers are there?+
34-35 million speakers: Kerala 30M, Tamil Nadu/Puducherry minorities 2M, diaspora USA/Gulf 2M+. Kerala has India's highest literacy rate (93.9%).
Why is Malayalam orthography unique?+
Malayalam preserves 15 vowels (exceptional among Indic scripts), uses tall vertical letters on single baseline (vs Tamil/Kannada centered), and employs highly stylized conjunct ligatures.
