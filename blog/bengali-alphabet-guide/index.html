About the Bengali Script

The Bengali script (Bangla lipi) descends from Brahmi through the Gupta and Siddham scripts, taking recognizable form under the Pala dynasty — the oldest surviving Bengali texts, the Charyapada mystic songs, date to the 10th–12th centuries. Modern printed Bengali was born in 1778, when punch-cutter Panchanan Karmakar cut the first Bengali movable type for Nathaniel Halhed's Grammar of the Bengal Language — decades before most Indian scripts were ever printed. The alphabet order taught today was standardized by Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar in his 1855 primer Barnaparichay, still the first book many Bengali children read. It is the script of Rabindranath Tagore, the first non-European Nobel laureate in Literature (1913), and the only script in the world whose poetry became the national anthem of two countries — India's Jana Gana Mana and Bangladesh's Amar Shonar Bangla, both written by Tagore in Bengali.

What Makes the Bengali Alphabet Distinctive

These are the features of the Bengali script that trip up learners coming from the Latin alphabet — the things worth understanding before you start reading or typing:

  • Bengali is an abugida, but its inherent vowel is ô (like the o in “hot”), not “a” — so ক is read “kô”, not “ka”. This one difference re-shapes how every consonant sounds compared with Hindi.
  • Bengali merged the three sibilants — শ, ষ and স are almost all pronounced “sh”, so the language has far fewer distinct s/sh sounds than its spelling suggests.
  • There’s no native v/w or z: ব covers b/v, and জ/য cover the j sound — foreign z and v words get borrowed into these.
  • Like Devanagari it hangs letters from a top line (matra), but Bengali’s letters have a distinctive rounded, curved look, and it shares its script with Assamese.
  • Special signs finish the system: ৎ (khonḍo to, a final t with no vowel), ং (anusvar, ‘ng’) and the nasalising chandrabindu ঁ.

Bengali Script — The Facts

  • Unicode block: Bengali, U+0980–09FF
  • ISO 15924 script code: Beng (325)
  • Script family: Brahmic abugida (Bengali–Assamese script)
  • First attested: ~11th century CE
  • Speakers: 270 million (Ethnologue)

Did You Know? Bengali numerals (০১২৩৪৫৬৭৮৯) are entirely different from both Arabic and Devanagari numerals. Bangladesh uses Bengali numerals officially, while West Bengal in India uses both Bengali and international numerals depending on context.

About the Bengali Language

Bengali is the seventh most spoken language in the world with 230 million speakers in Bangladesh and India's West Bengal. February 21st — International Mother Language Day — originated from the Bengali Language Movement of 1952, when students died defending the right to use Bengali. The Avro phonetic keyboard system is widely used for Bengali digital input.

Ekushey February — When a Language Became a Global Symbol

On February 21, 1952, students in Dhaka were killed by Pakistani police while protesting for the right to use Bengali as an official language. This event became the foundation of Bangladesh's independence movement and inspired UNESCO to declare February 21 as International Mother Language Day — celebrated worldwide. For Bengali speakers, the language carries a weight of sacrifice and identity that few other languages share. Frequently Asked Questions

How do I type juktakkhor (যুক্তাক্ষর) — conjunct letters like ক্ত or ন্ড?+
In phonetic mode, type the consonant sounds together — "rakta" produces রক্ত with the ক্ত conjunct formed automatically. On the visual layout, press the first consonant, the hasanta (্), then the second: ক + ্ + ত = ক্ত. Bengali has hundreds of these fused forms, and the keyboard outputs clean Unicode so every font renders them correctly.
Why does the vowel sign appear to the left of the letter I just typed?+
That's correct Bengali behavior, not a bug. The e-kar (ে) and oi-kar (ৈ) always render to the LEFT of their consonant even though they are typed after it: ক then ে displays as কে. The o-kar (ো) and ou-kar (ৌ) even split around both sides. Unicode stores the letters in phonetic order and the font performs the visual reordering.
Is this the same as typing with Avro?+
The phonetic principle is the same — write Bengali sounds in Latin letters ("Banglish") and get Bangla script. Avro is a program you install on Windows; Kactyl runs in the browser on any device with nothing to download — which matters on a work computer, an internet café in the Gulf, or a borrowed phone.
What is Banglish, and can this keyboard convert it?+
Banglish is Bengali written in Latin letters — "kemon acho" instead of কেমন আছো — the way most Bengalis type in chat apps because their phone ships with an English keyboard. Kactyl's phonetic mode is built exactly for this habit: keep typing the Banglish you already know, and the output is proper Bangla script that renders correctly for the person receiving it.
