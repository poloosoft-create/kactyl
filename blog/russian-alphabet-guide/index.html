About the Russian Script

Russian uses Cyrillic script, adopted from Old Church Slavonic around 10th century CE (same era as Bulgarian, Serbian). Kyivan Rus developed its own Cyrillic variant by 11th-12th centuries. Tatar/Mongol occupations (13th-15th centuries) did not eliminate Cyrillic — Russian literary tradition persisted in churches/monasteries. 17th century Russian reforms standardized orthography under Peter the Great. Soviet era (20th century) simplified some Cyrillic letters but retained core system. Modern Russian orthography standardized 1956 with etymological spelling (not phonemic, unlike Bulgarian) that preserves historical word origins.

What Makes the Russian Alphabet Distinctive

These are the features of the Russian script that trip up learners coming from the Latin alphabet — the things worth understanding before you start reading or typing:

  • Cyrillic has “false friends” that look Latin but sound different: В is v (not b), Н is n (not h), Р is r (not p), С is s (not c), У is oo.
  • Two letters make no sound of their own: the hard sign Ъ and the soft sign Ь only change how the consonant before them is pronounced (softening it or keeping it hard).
  • Single Cyrillic letters capture whole English clusters: Ж = zh, Ц = ts, Ч = ch, Ш = sh, Щ = shch.
  • Ё is usually written as plain Е in everyday text (the two dots dropped), even though it changes the pronunciation — a famous source of confusion.
  • Cyrillic was created in the 9th century from Greek uppercase letters — you can still see it in А, В, Е, К, М, О, Р, Т.

About the Russian Language

Russian is the most widely spoken Slavic language with 150 million native speakers and 260 million total speakers. It uses the 33-letter Cyrillic alphabet. VK (VKontakte) and Telegram are Russia's dominant social platforms; Telegram was founded by Russians and has over 700 million global users. The informal practice of writing Russian in Latin letters (translit) is called 'Russinglish' or 'translit' and was common before Cyrillic keyboards became standard on phones.

Russian Translit — From SMS Necessity to Internet Aesthetic

Before smartphones, typing Cyrillic on Latin-keyboard feature phones required either special software or translit — writing Russian words phonetically in Latin letters. 'Privet' for привет, 'spasibo' for спасибо, 'kak dela' for как дела. This translit culture left a lasting imprint on Russian internet communication: even today, some Russian internet communities use translit as an aesthetic choice or for humor. Modern Russian speakers in Western countries often use phones set to English or local languages, making a browser-based Cyrillic keyboard valuable for maintaining Russian correspondence with family. The Cyrillic alphabet has 33 letters including several with no Latin equivalent: Ё (yo), Ж (zh), Щ (shch), Ъ (hard sign), Ы, Ь (soft sign), Э, Ю, Я. These require phonetic mapping for translit input.

Frequently Asked Questions

What script does Russian use?+
Russian uses Cyrillic script, adopted from Old Church Slavonic ~10th century. Modern orthography standardized 1956. Russian Cyrillic uses 33 letters (different from Bulgarian 30, Serbian 34, Ukrainian 32).
How is Russian different from Bulgarian?+
Russian uses etymological spelling (historical origin); Bulgarian uses phonemic spelling (as pronounced). Russian has 6 cases; Bulgarian eliminated cases. Stress is mobile (word-specific) in both.
How many Russian speakers are there?+
258-288 million speakers: Russia 154M, diaspora 3M+, Soviet-successor states 50M+ as L2. Ranks 4th most-spoken language globally by native speakers.
What is Russian etymological spelling?+
Russian words preserve historical/etymological spelling even when pronunciation changed. Example: "что" (what) pronounced "shto" but written with original letters. This differs from phonemic scripts.
