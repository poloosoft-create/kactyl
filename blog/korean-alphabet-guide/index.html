About the Korean Script

Korean used Chinese characters (Hanzi/Hanja) until 15th century. King Sejong the Great invented Hangul script (1443) — specifically designed alphabetic system to make reading/writing accessible to common people (not just scholars). Hangul proven more efficient than Chinese characters: 24 letters (14 consonants, 10 vowels) designed with scientific precision. Hangul competed with Hanja for centuries; Japanese occupation (1910-1945) suppressed Hangul. Post-1945 independence elevated Hangul as national script. Modern orthography standardized 1933, with reforms 1988. Hangul declared UNESCO Memory of the World (1997).

What Makes the Korean Alphabet Distinctive

These are the features of the Korean script that trip up learners coming from the Latin alphabet — the things worth understanding before you start reading or typing:

  • Hangul letters aren’t written in a line — they’re packed into square syllable blocks, one block per syllable: ㅎ + ㅏ + ㄴ combine into 한 (han).
  • Hangul is one of the few scripts with a known inventor and date: King Sejong the Great, 1443. The consonant shapes were deliberately designed to mimic the shape of the mouth and tongue making each sound.
  • The letter ㅇ is silent at the start of a syllable (a placeholder so every syllable begins with a consonant slot) but is pronounced ng at the end.
  • Consonants come in three series — plain (ㄱ ㄷ ㅂ), tense (ㄲ ㄸ ㅃ) and aspirated (ㅋ ㅌ ㅍ) — the difference is how much air and tension you use.
  • Often called the most logical writing system ever designed, Hangul is famously learnable in a single afternoon.

Korean Script — The Facts

  • Unicode block: Hangul Jamo U+1100; Syllables U+AC00–D7AF
  • ISO 15924 script code: Hang (286)
  • Script family: Featural alphabet, invented 1443
  • First attested: 1443 CE (Hunminjeongeum)
  • Speakers: 80 million (Ethnologue)

Did You Know? Korean is one of only a handful of languages in the world where we know the exact date of the writing system's creation: October 9, 1443 (now celebrated as Hangul Day in South Korea, a national holiday). Most alphabets evolved over centuries with no single inventor — Hangul's deliberate design makes it exceptional.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is Hangul?+
Hangul (한글) is Korean alphabet invented 1443 by King Sejong. Scientific design: 14 consonants, 10 vowels shaped to represent mouth positions. UNESCO Memory of the World (1997). Modern Korean orthography standardized 1933, reformed 1988.
Why did King Sejong invent Hangul?+
Korean used Chinese characters (Hanja) but they were complex. Sejong invented Hangul to make literacy accessible to common people, not just scholars. Scientifically designed for Korean language phonetics.
How many speakers does Korean have?+
81-82 million speakers: South Korea 51M, North Korea 26M, diaspora USA 1.8M, China/Japan minorities 2M+. Korean unified language despite political division.
What are Korean consonant shapes?+
Korean consonants visually represent mouth positions: ㄴ (tongue behind teeth), ㄱ (tongue position), ㅁ (lips). Consonant shape tells pronunciation mechanics — unique feature among world scripts.
