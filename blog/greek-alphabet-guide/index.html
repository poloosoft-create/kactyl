About the Greek Script

The Greek alphabet was formally standardized in 403 BCE when Athens adopted the Ionian alphabet under archon Eucleides, replacing the Old Attic alphabet during post-Thirty Tyrants democratic reforms. By the end of the 4th century BCE, the Eucleidean alphabet had displaced local Greek variants to become the universal standard across Greek-speaking regions. In the modern era, 1976 marked the official standardization of Modern Greek orthography, establishing Demotic Greek (Δημοτική) over Katharevousa as the standard for education, administration, and literature. 1982 brought a diacritical system simplification in a follow-up official reform, creating the monochord (single accent) system used in Modern Greek keyboards today.

What Makes the Greek Alphabet Distinctive

These are the features of the Greek script that trip up learners coming from the Latin alphabet — the things worth understanding before you start reading or typing:

  • Modern Greek sounds differ from the classical values learned in maths and science: β is “v” (not b), δ is a soft “th” (this), θ is a hard “th” (thin), and η, ι, υ all sound “ee”.
  • Sigma is the only letter with two lowercase forms: σ inside a word, but ς only at the very end (τέλος).
  • Greek is the ancestor of the Latin alphabet (Α→A, Β→B, Ε→E) and the source of countless science symbols — π, Σ, Δ, Ω, λ.
  • It has seven vowels (α ε η ι ο υ ω), but several now sound identical — the three “ee” letters (η, ι, υ) are kept apart only by historical spelling.
  • The Greek alphabet is the oldest still in continuous use (~2,800 years) and the first writing system to give vowels their own full letters.

About the Greek Language

Greek is spoken by 13 million people in Greece and Cyprus. The Greek alphabet is the ancestor of all European alphabets — Latin, Cyrillic, and others. 'Greeklish' (typing Greek in Latin letters) emerged on early mobile phones and is still common in informal contexts. Facebook and Instagram are the dominant Greek social platforms.

The Alphabet's Alphabet — Greek's 3,000-Year Legacy

The word 'alphabet' comes from the first two Greek letters: alpha (Α) and beta (Β). The Greek writing system, developed around 800 BCE from Phoenician script, is the ancestor of the Latin alphabet (A B C...), the Cyrillic alphabet (А Б В...), and many others. When you type in English, you're using letters derived from Greek. Greek itself has been continuously written for 3,000 years — making it the world's oldest continuously written language in active use. Modern Greek evolved from ancient Greek through Byzantine Greek, and educated Greeks can often understand ancient Greek texts from 2,500 years ago with some study. The 'Greeklish' practice — writing Greek words in Latin letters — emerged when Greek mobile phones lacked Cyrillic input and is still used informally. Frequently Asked Questions

What is the monochord diacritical system?+
Modern Greek (post-1982) uses only ONE accent mark (΄) to mark word stress on vowels. Classical Greek had multiple marks (acute, grave, circumflex) with different meanings. Modern Greek simplified this to just the stress accent — much easier to type and learn.
How do I type accented Greek vowels?+
Use the semicolon key followed by the vowel: ;a = ά, ;e = έ, ;i = ί, ;o = ό, ;u = ύ. This phonetic method works on any Latin QWERTY keyboard without installing Greek layout software.
What is the difference between Modern Greek and Ancient Greek?+
Modern Greek (Δημοτική) is the living language spoken today with 13M+ native speakers. Ancient Greek (used in Homer, Plato, Aristotle) is a historical language with zero native speakers today — studied in universities. Typing support focuses entirely on Modern Greek.
How many letters does the Greek alphabet have?+
The Greek alphabet has 24 letters (Α-Ω uppercase, α-ω lowercase). This is fewer than Latin's 26, and Greek has no uppercase/lowercase variants that look completely different like Devanagari or Arabic.
