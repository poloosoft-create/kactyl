About the Persian Script

Persian adopted the Arabic alphabet after the Arab conquest of Iran between 636 and 651 CE, replacing the Pahlavi script used since the Sassanid Empire. By the 10th century, Persian scholars had formalized four new letters absent from Arabic: پ (pe), چ (che), ژ (zhe), and گ (gaf), bringing the alphabet to 32 letters. Ferdowsi's Shahnameh (~977–1010 CE) — the world's longest epic poem at ~50,000 couplets — deliberately avoided Arabic loanwords, which is why Persian today retains a vocabulary distinct from Arabic. This script became the writing template for Urdu, Pashto, Sindhi, and Uyghur through Persian's literary dominance.

What Makes the Persian Alphabet Distinctive

These are the features of the Persian script that trip up learners coming from the Latin alphabet — the things worth understanding before you start reading or typing:

  • Persian uses the Arabic script but adds four letters Arabic doesn't have, for sounds Persian needed: پ (p), چ (ch), ژ (zh), and گ (g).
  • Several letters share one sound. ز، ذ، ض، ظ are all pronounced z; س، ص، ث are all s; and ت، ط are both t — a legacy of Arabic loanwords whose original spelling Persian kept even though the sounds merged.
  • Persian’s ک (keheh, U+06A9) and ی (Farsi yeh, U+06CC) are shaped differently from their Arabic cousins — the Farsi yeh drops its two dots when it stands alone or ends a word. This is exactly why a Persian page must use a Persian-capable font, not a plain Arabic one.
  • Like Arabic, short vowels are usually left unwritten; the three long vowels are carried by ا (â), و (u) and ی (i), and readers supply the rest from context.
  • There are no capital letters, and the script is cursive — most letters change shape depending on whether they sit at the start, middle, or end of a word.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is Finglish and how did it develop?+
Finglish (also Pinglish) is Persian written phonetically in Latin letters — "salam" for سلام, "chetori" for چطوری. It emerged in the 1990s on early chat platforms and SMS where Persian Unicode was not supported, and persisted in diaspora communities where many never learned the Persian alphabet. Our keyboard includes a Finglish mode: type in English phonetics and the keyboard converts to correct Persian script in real time, including the four letters unique to Persian (پ چ ژ گ) that have no standard Latin equivalent.
What are the 4 Persian letters not found in Arabic?+
Persian adds four letters to the 28-letter Arabic alphabet: پ (pe, as in "pen"), چ (che, as in "chair"), ژ (zhe, as in "measure"), and گ (gaf, hard "g" as in "go"). These were created by adding dots to existing Arabic letterforms and standardized by the 10th century to represent sounds that exist in Persian but not in Arabic. They bring the Persian alphabet to 32 letters total.
What is the difference between Farsi Yeh (ی) and Arabic Yeh (ي)?+
Both letters look similar in initial and medial positions, but differ in their final and isolated forms. Farsi Yeh (ی, U+06CC) — used in standard Persian — has no dots underneath when it appears at the end of a word. Arabic Yeh (ي, U+0649) keeps its two dots in all positions. Many keyboards and autocorrect systems substitute one for the other silently. Iranian readers immediately notice the difference — it is one of the most common sources of incorrectly rendered Persian text online.
What is the Zero-Width Non-Joiner (ZWNJ) and why does Persian need it?+
ZWNJ (U+200C) is an invisible character that prevents adjacent letters from joining in a cursive script. Persian connects letters within words, but compound words often need a visual break between their parts without a visible space. For example, می‌رود (mirávad — "he goes") uses a ZWNJ between the prefix می and verb رود so they stay visually separated. Copy-pasting through systems that strip ZWNJ collapses these words and makes them grammatically ambiguous.
