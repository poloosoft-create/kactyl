About the Marathi Script

Marathi has been written for over a thousand years — but not always in today's script. For centuries, everyday Marathi ran in Modi, a cursive script developed for fast administrative writing under the Maratha empire; Devanagari (in its Balbodh form) was reserved for scholarship. The British-era printing press tipped the balance, and by the early 20th century Balbodh Devanagari became standard while Modi faded into archives. Marathi Devanagari is not identical to Hindi's: it keeps the letter ळ, pronounces ज्ञ as 'dnya', uses the eyelash reph (ऱ्य), and adopted ॲ and ऑ for English vowels.

What Makes the Marathi Alphabet Distinctive

These are the features of the Marathi script that trip up learners coming from the Latin alphabet — the things worth understanding before you start reading or typing:

  • Marathi is written in Devanagari but adds a letter Hindi doesn’t use: ळ (ḷa), a retroflex “L” heard in everyday words like कमळ (lotus) and शाळा (school).
  • Unlike Hindi, Marathi usually pronounces the inherent “a” at the end of a word — राम is “Rāma” in Marathi, not the clipped “Rām” of Hindi.
  • Marathi keeps older Sanskrit letters and the vocalic ऋ more actively than modern Hindi, reflecting its heavy Sanskrit vocabulary.
  • Marathi typography has its own quirk — the “eyelash reph” (र्‍), a special half-r form you won’t see in Hindi.
  • It shares Devanagari’s abugida structure and top line (shirorekha) with Hindi, so the letters look familiar even though the language is different.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is Marathi Devanagari different from Hindi's?+
Same script family, different toolkit. Marathi keeps ळ — a retroflex 'l' Hindi doesn't use — in everyday words like बाळ (child), कमळ (lotus) and खेळ (game). Marathi reads ज्ञ as 'dnya' (ज्ञान = dnyaan), uses the 'eyelash' form of र in words like ऱ्हास and वऱ्हाड, and writes English vowels with ॲ and ऑ (बॅट, ऑफिस). A Hindi keyboard can type most Marathi letters, but it won't know Marathi spelling habits — this keyboard does.
What is the Modi script — did Marathi always use Devanagari?+
No — for roughly 600 years, everyday Marathi was written in Modi, a fast cursive script used for Maratha administration, land records and letters, while Balbodh Devanagari was for scholarship. Printing presses standardized on Devanagari, and by the early 1900s Modi retired to the archives. Millions of historical documents still wait in Modi — it's a living research field — but modern Marathi, and this keyboard, use Balbodh Devanagari.
How do I type the letter ळ?+
ळ has its own key on this layout — no workaround needed. In phonetic mode, type 'L' (capital) or 'La' where the retroflex is meant: बाळ (baaL — child), कमळ (kamaL — lotus), टाळी (taaLee — clap). It's one of the letters that instantly marks text as Marathi rather than Hindi, so getting it right matters for both readers and search.
How do I type half letters and conjuncts like ज्ञ, श्र, क्त?+
Type the first consonant, the halant (्), then the next: क + ् + त = क्त. Common fused forms — ज्ञ, श्र, त्र, क्ष — build automatically in phonetic mode: 'dnyan' produces ज्ञान, 'shri' produces श्री. Marathi also uses the eyelash reph for र before य and ह: type र + ् + zero-width-joiner + य and the keyboard handles the sequence — ऱ्या renders with the eyelash, not the hook.
